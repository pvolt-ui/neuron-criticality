June · the proposal

What I set out to find

senses

the middle of the brain → descending neurons
commands to the body
  • Hypothesis: some interneurons are bottlenecks — “if you removed them, information could no longer get through.”
  • Payoff question: is any neuron a bottleneck for all three senses? That would be a genuinely critical hub of the whole brain.
  • Why it matters: in human brain imaging, network hubs are the regions preferentially damaged in Alzheimer’s and stroke. The fly is the only complete wiring diagram where the idea can be tested exactly.

Both predictions get tested directly in this talk — and both will turn out to be wrong in an interesting way.

mid-July · first result, first mistake

DNp32 — a headline that wasn’t real

  • First full run used unweighted betweenness — every connection counted the same — and produced a headline: DNp32, a bottleneck shared by smell and vision.
  • The problem: the edge list I had inherited carried no synapse counts. A 5-synapse whisper and a 500-synapse highway looked identical.
  • With the real weighted Codex data, DNp32’s betweenness is exactly zero in both pathways. It sits on no strong route at all — a pure artifact.
Weighting is not a refinement — it decides the answer. An ablation isolating the weighting shows it alone changes 23–32 of each top 50. After this, no result in the project went unreported without a control.

early August

Rebuilt on weighted data — the anatomy checks out

  • From connectivity alone, the ranking recovers textbook anatomy: olfactory bottlenecks concentrate in the antennal lobe (the first smell relay), touch bottlenecks in the gnathal ganglion (the touch→motor integration centre).
  • Cross-modal check: 5 neurons appear in two pathways’ top 50, none in all three — the first crack in the hypothesis.

the week everything got tested

Is “important” even well-defined?

  • Different importance metrics pick almost completely different neurons: betweenness vs. the BANC influence metric overlap at 1/50 and 0/50.
  • Worse: in two of three pathways, betweenness largely reduces to counting synapses (23/50, 26/50 overlap with raw synapse count).
  • Rescue: a null that shuffles the network while preserving every neuron’s exact degree — 200 trials, FDR-corrected — 37–45 of 50 survive. The top neurons matter because of where they sit, not how many synapses they have.

the deletion test

The hypothesis fails

  • Deleted each top neuron and re-solved every sensory→motor route: routes get under 1% longer on average, and zero source→target pairs are ever disconnected.
  • The five cross-modal neurons are indistinguishable from ordinary top-50 neurons (z between −0.79 and +0.94).
  • Growth note: I also caught my own scoring artifact here — deleting a neuron that is itself an endpoint trivially cuts its own pairs, which had inflated an earlier version of this result.

replication · the strongest result

It reproduces in a second brain

  • Entire pipeline rerun on the Janelia male CNS — different animal, different sex, different reconstruction pipeline.
  • Cell-type rankings agree at ρ = 0.45–0.71; top-25 overlaps are 4.5–43× chance. The redundancy result and the degree null replicate too.
  • Whatever betweenness measures here, it is a property of the fly nervous system, not of one reconstruction.

  • Betweenness ranks traffic. Necessity is a property of populations. T4 cells are individually redundant on any single path — exactly what scores low on betweenness and high on necessity when the whole population is blocked.

synthesis

What I think the story is now

structured The junctions are real: they beat degree-preserving nulls and reproduce across two animals.

but redundant No single point of failure anywhere — the pathway routes around every deletion.

and metric-dependent Which neurons get called “critical” depends heavily on the metric you choose.

The proposal’s core prediction — remove a bottleneck and information stops — was tested directly and is wrong. I’d rather report that with controls than the reverse without them.

The BANC question

  • Bates et al. (July 31) reach a compatible distributed-control conclusion on BANC, using their influence metric.
  • But their paper contains no path-based metrics at all — no betweenness, no centrality, no shortest paths — and no deletion tests, no nulls, no cross-dataset ranking comparison. My metric-disagreement result could not exist inside their framework.

In reserve for questions

  • Null A (weight permutation): real weights displace rankings further than permuted ones, z = +2.6 to +4.5, all pathways.
  • Cohort sweep: deleting whole cell types is mostly additive (median 1.02–1.14× the sum of single deletions); a small minority (VA2_adPN, 3.9×) are genuinely more than their parts.
  • Current-flow betweenness abandoned: 24 h unfinished on the smallest graph, and it requires an undirected graph — signal running backwards from motor to eye.
  • Motor endpoint (male CNS): at 2 hops only mechanosensory reaches real motor neurons (4,961 sources); olfactory and visual reach essentially none — which retroactively justifies the descending-neuron endpoint.
